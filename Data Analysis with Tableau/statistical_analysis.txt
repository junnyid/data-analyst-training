Histograms
    - A histogram is a chart that groups continuous values into different groups (known as bins) to show the distribution of numerical data
    - Histograms are univariate visualizations, meaning they only plot one numerical field
    - When a histogram is created, a dimension called a bin created, which is used within the visual.
    - Bins describe a consistent value range that data is sorted into to from groups
    - To interpret a histogram, you should be aware of skewness. Skewness is the measure of symmetry or asymmetry of a distribution
    Distributions are typically classified as left-skewed, right-skewed, or balanced:
        + Left skewed (negatively skewed)
        + Right skewed (positively skewed)
        + Balanced (symmetrical)
    - A few questions you can answer using histogram:
        + what is the distribution of salaries at the company?
        + What is the distribution of product costs?
        + What is the distribution of the Superstore sales?

    Histogram Design
        - Histogram Dos and Don'ts 
            + DO: choose the right bin size; consider the shape and distribution
            + DON'T: use inconsistent intervals; include too many bins; Leave gaps for continuos data; forget to try different bin counts

Box Plots
    - Box plots, also knowns as box-and-whisker plots, are visualizations that show a statistical summary of selected data. 
    - The statistical summary is made up of five values: the data's minimum, maximm, median, first quartile, and third quartile.
    - Box plots give a quick overview of the data within a field, which can be useful for explaining your analysis
    - A box plot provides:
        + Minimum: The smallest value in the dataset.
        + Maximum: The largest value in the dataset. 
        + Lower Whisker: The smallest value within the dataset that falls within an acceptable range, typically within 1.5 times the interquartile range (IQR) <phạm vi liên vùng> below the first quartile (Q1) 
                        => If the value falls out of this range, it will be considered a potential outlier
        + Upper Whisker: The largest value within the dataset that falls within an acceptable range, typically within 1.5 times the interquartile range (IQR) above the third quartile (Q3)
                        => If the value falls out of this range, it will be considered a potential outlier
        + Quartiles: Numbers that divide the values in your datase:
            * Lower Hinge/ First quartile (Q1)
            * Median (Q2)
            * Upper Hinge/ Third quartile (Q3)
        + Interquatile range (IQR): phạm vi liên vùng
    - A few questions: 
        * What is the distribution of waiting times for customers at different service counters?
        * How do the ages of participants differ across different categories of a survey?
        * What is the variability in monthly sales for different products in a retail store?
    - Box Plot Design
        + Box Plot Dos and Don'ts
            * DO: Use the right data; include and explain outliers; use annotations; assess symmetry
            * DON'T: Use for limited data points; Use when measurements are similar; Ignore scale

Custom Analytics
    - Analytics is the process of discovering and interpreting meaning from data. 
    - It is the application of a variety of disciplines such as statistics, computational mathematics, and modeling to discover patterns and draw reliable conclusions.
    - Reference Lines: straight lines that are either vertical or horizontal and show a comparision between each data point on an axis and the value of the references line itself
    - Reference Bands: similar to reference lines, but they also use a secondary number and shade the area between two values. The shaded can be useful for showing which data points fit within a specific range or area
    - Distribution Bands: a visual representation of the distribution of data points within a given range or interval
                        They help users understand the concentration or density of values across a continuous axis, typically used for numerical data
                        These bands showcase the spread of data by indicating the frequency or density of values within specific ranges.
